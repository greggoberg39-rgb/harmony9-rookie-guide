<p><em><strong>Article By: Ben Giumarra, Spillane Consulting Associates</strong></em></p>
<p>Here&#8217;s what you need to know about avoiding RESPA Section 8 anti-kickback violations when sponsoring a party for real estate agents or other referral partners.</p>

<p class="font_8">The industry has adopted more conservative stances towards RESPA Section 8 anti-kickback concerns since the CFPB came in and ramped up enforcement in this area. But just as some of those decisions may have been over-conservative despite an active CFPB, some activities may remain too aggressive even under what we expect might be a more relaxed enforcement attitude. Of course, the CFPB isn&#8217;t the only organization capable of enforcing this rule &#8211; state and other Federal agencies may very well continue what the CFPB has started, even if the CFPB itself changes direction.</p>
<p class="font_8"><strong>RESPA Section 8 prohibits giving any &#8220;thing of value&#8221; (does not have to be cold hard cash) in exchange for the referral of mortgage-related business (this agreement can be express or implied)</strong>. So what if I want to host an event for a group of real estate agents (or other referral partners, like divorce attorneys, accountants, builders, etc.)? If this benefits them, does this alone mean I&#8217;m in trouble?<strong> No</strong>. <em>If you&#8217;re in the mortgage business, you can- and probably should- be hosting such events</em>. But here are some thoughts on how you can do this without getting yourself or your company into hot water&#8230;</p>
<p class="font_8"><strong>Let&#8217;s Have a Party!</strong></p>
<p class="font_8">Here&#8217;s the situation. You&#8217;re planning a Christmas party to celebrate this past year&#8217;s success and as a thank you to everyone who contributed to it. Naturally, you&#8217;re going to invite real estate agents and other people you do business with. There will be food, drinks, a raffle, music, etc. You are considering holding it at a nice restaurant. The problem is that you also invited your compliance guy, from Spillane Consulting, and he&#8217;s bringing his date, an aid for Senator Elizabeth Warren.</p>
<p class="font_8">Now you&#8217;re thinking &#8230; is this okay?? Well &#8230; it depends. It can be! But you&#8217;re definitely providing &#8220;things of value&#8221; to people who refer mortgage business to you, so we at least have to think about avoiding RESPA concerns.</p>
<p class="font_8"><strong>Two Ways to Be Safe Here</strong></p>
<p class="font_8"><strong>#1 &#8211; Normal Promotional Activities&#8221;</strong></p>
<p class="font_8">The first way to be safe here is if you can classify this event as a &#8220;normal promotional activity&#8221; that <strong>does &#8220;not involve the defray of expenses that otherwise would be incurred by&#8221; attendees.</strong> This is a protective <em><strong>provision from RESPA that says such activities cannot be illegal kickbacks</strong></em>, so the fact that you&#8217;re giving &#8220;things of value&#8221; to referral partners isn&#8217;t a problem.</p>
<p class="font_8">How can you qualify this party as &#8220;normal promotional activities&#8221;? Hint: Inviting only people who actually referred you loans, giving out Rolex watches as party favors, and holding a raffle with a $5,000 value where attendees receive one ticket for every referral from the past year, is not going to count as &#8220;normal promotional activities.&#8221; Similarly, paying for a group of real estate agents to attend a party that you&#8217;re paying for but where there is no one representing your company (i.e. no one to promote your company) won&#8217;t cut it either.</p>
<p class="font_8">Here are some ideas instead that actually would help:</p>
<ul class="font_8">
<li>
<p class="font_8">Distribute informational brochures about your company</p>
</li>
<li>
<p class="font_8">Give a presentation on the products/services you plan to offer for next year.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p class="font_8">Have the party well attended by representatives from your company</p>
</li>
<li>
<p class="font_8">Avoiding offering things such as CE credits that attendees would have had to pay for by themselves otherwise (don&#8217;t defray expenses)</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p class="font_8">It&#8217;s kind of a pain to have to &#8220;watch our backs&#8221; for later updates to the demographic data. It would certainly be preferable to just &#8220;lock&#8221; the demographic data as of the time of application and forget about it.</p>
<p class="font_8"><b>#2 &#8211; No Agreement to Refer (Express or Implied)</b></p>
<p class="font_8">OK. So there also can&#8217;t be a RESPA Section 8 violation unless an &#8220;agreement&#8221; to exchange a kickback for referral can be shown. The problem is in proving that there isn&#8217;t some form of implied agreement in certain cases. Imagine if you give your top referral partners Superbowl tickets &#8211; Can you demonstrate that this wasn&#8217;t in expectation for referrals? Maybe prove that you&#8217;re just really really good friends?</p>
<p class="font_8">But there are some steps you can take to demonstrate that there is no improper agreement in place. One idea is to simply <strong>invite more people!</strong>  Don&#8217;t limit invitations to referral partners &#8211; opening the party up to everyone in the real estate community would be safer, removing the requirement that they referred a certain number of loans to you in the past year. Now, you don&#8217;t necessarily need to take this to the absolute extreme &#8211; but definitely want to avoid invitations that say &#8220;Anyone who has referred more than 5 mortgage loans to us is invited.&#8221; Just avoid referencing referrals and related things altogether!</p>

<p class="font_8"><strong>On My Mind &#8230;</strong></p>
<p class="font_8">Many will argue- myself included- that <strong>prioritizing other people&#8217;s interests over your own</strong> is, counter-intuitively, probably the best way to advance your own interests. And that&#8217;s especially true in this industry as I know it to be, where decent and kind people value long-term relationships over short-term gains.</p>
<p class="font_8">But if you agree with me, do you ever find yourself describing a scenario as &#8220;win-win&#8221;? According to the The Go-Giver by Bob Burg and David Mann, that you may be losing sight of your goal to selflessly help</p>
<p class="font_8">others &#8230;</p>
<p class="font_8"><em>Don&#8217;t keep track. That&#8217;s not networking &#8211; that&#8217;s poker. You know how people say &#8216;win-win&#8217;? &#8230;. [M]ost of the time, what people call &#8216;win-win&#8217; is really just a disguised way of keeping track. Making sure we all come out even, that nobody gets the advantage. Even-Steven. I scratched your back, so now you owe me. &#8230; When you base your relationships &#8211; in business or anywhere else in your life &#8211; on who owes who what, that&#8217;s not being a friend. That&#8217;s being a creditor. </em></p>
<p class="font_8">And that&#8217;s one thing I admire so much about our industry &#8211; time and again someone will help me in some meaningful way with absolutely zero interest in their own interests, or in finding a &#8220;win-win&#8221; arrangement. It&#8217;s still a little uncomfortable for me because I don&#8217;t think I&#8217;m helping others as much as they&#8217;re helping me &#8230; but hopefully with all this support I&#8217;ll be around plenty long enough to do something about it. 🙂</p>
